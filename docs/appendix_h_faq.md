# Appendix H — Frequently Asked Questions

## 1. When is GAD overkill?

When work is small, reversible, non-sensitive, non-production, and carries little external consequence, use proportional governance. You can adopt GAD's authority/evidence principles without the full control stack.

## 2. Does GAD replace Agile, Waterfall, SAFe, ITSM, or enterprise governance?

No. GAD is an integration and operating model. It can wrap or complement existing delivery and governance systems.

## 3. Can high AI confidence grant A2, A3, or A4 authority?

No. Confidence may trigger more or less review inside an already authorized policy, but it never creates authority.

## 4. Can A4 ever be justified?

Yes, but A4 is the strongest control category for high-impact, irreversible, or authority-changing actions. It requires the applicable human authority and evidence. It is not synonymous with full AI autonomy.

## 5. What if a human and an AI disagree?

The AI records its evidence, uncertainty, and recommendation. The applicable human authority decides when human judgment is required. The disagreement and decision remain traceable.

## 6. What happens if a vendor model changes mid-pilot?

Record the effective model identity, assess whether the change affects validation assumptions, and revalidate the affected capability when material.

## 7. Can one AI agent authorize another?

No. AI may hand off work, but authorization must come from the applicable human/policy authority and be valid for the receiving agent.

## 8. What if a mandatory control fails?

The affected boundary fails closed: stop, preserve state/evidence, classify the blocker, remediate/escalate, and resume only after the mandatory condition is satisfied.

## 9. Does remediation erase a historical failure?

No. The original result remains. A remediation creates a new result and may change current readiness.

## 10. How does GAD scale without creating approval bottlenecks?

By automating stable controls, improving evidence packaging, using explicit A2 standing delegations for low-risk repeatable work, and reserving human attention for consequential decisions.
